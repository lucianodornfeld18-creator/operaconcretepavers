# Part 2: Florida law for concrete, pavers and artificial turf (checked 2026-10-01)

Method: the statute text comes from flsenate.gov (the 2026 Florida Statutes, compared against the 2025 edition). The rule text comes from flrules.org and from the Cornell LII copy of the Florida Administrative Code. Anything I did not read on a fetched page is listed under UNVERIFIED.

## 1. Licensing: is a state license needed for concrete, flatwork or paver work?

- Definition of "contractor" (F.S. 489.105(3)): a person who, for compensation, will "construct, repair, alter, remodel, add to, demolish, subtract from, or improve any building or structure, including related improvements to real estate," and "whose job scope is substantially similar to the job scope described in one of the paragraphs of this subsection." Division I covers paragraphs (a)-(c): general, building and residential. Division II covers paragraphs (d)-(q). (F.S. 489.105 (2026) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.105)
- A "Specialty contractor" (489.105(3)(q)) is a contractor whose scope is "limited to a particular phase of construction established in a category adopted by board rule." A "Certified contractor" holds a state certificate of competency, so it can work anywhere in the state. A "Registered contractor" is registered with the state on the basis of a local competency license and can work only in the jurisdictions where it is licensed. (F.S. 489.105 (2026) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.105); (F.S. 489.117(1)(b) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.117)
- No statute paragraph names "concrete," "flatwork," "pavers," "driveway" or "retaining wall" as a contractor category. The 2026 edition of 489.105 differs from 2025 only by new definitions (20) "Subcontractor" and (21) "Supplier." (F.S. 489.105 (2026) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.105)
- **There IS a state-certified specialty category that covers concrete.** Rule 61G4-15.100(g) creates the "structural masonry specialty contractor," certified "to form, place, finish, install, repair, maintain, and replace any concrete or masonry products … the construction of forms … reinforcing steel and the pouring, placement, injecting and finishing of concrete. The scope of such work shall include all types of foundations, slabs, footers, curbs, walls, columns, beams and other structures." The rule was adopted effective 5/5/2024 and amended effective 8/28/2024. The same rule also creates a "structural pre-stressed, precast concrete work specialty contractor" and several marine categories (seawall, bulkhead, dock). (Fla. Admin. Code R. 61G4-15.100 (LII) — https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61G4-15-100); (FLRules 61G4-15.100 — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/ruleNo.asp?id=61G4-15.100)
- Rule 61G4-15.015 covers the "Specialty Structure" contractor (aluminum and allied work). It may build "foundations, slabs and block kneewalls incidental to" that work, but "shall not perform any work that alters the structural integrity of the building including … load bearing walls and foundations." (Fla. Admin. Code R. 61G4-15.015 (LII) — https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61G4-15-015)
- Rule 61G4-15.032 covers pool specialty contractors. The "Swimming Pool Structural Specialty Contractor" is limited to "the shaping and shooting of gunite, shotcrete, concrete, or similar product mix used in the construction of swimming pools and spas." These specialty contractors must work under contract and supervision of, and within the scope of, a contractor licensed under 489.105(3)(j)-(l). I read this from a search-result summary of the rule and did not open the rule text. (FLRules ch. 61G4-15 index — https://flrules.org/gateway/ChapterHome.asp?Chapter=61G4-15)
- **Driveways are expressly carved out (F.S. 489.117(4)(a)1.):** "A local government … may not require a person to obtain a license, issued by the local government or the state, for a job scope which does not substantially correspond to" a 489.105 category or a certified specialty category. "A local government may not require a state or local license to obtain a permit for such job scopes." The listed scopes include "painting; flooring; cabinetry; interior remodeling when the scope of the project does not include a task for which a state license is required; **driveway or tennis court installation**; handyman services; **decorative stone, tile, marble, granite, or terrazzo installation**; plastering; pressure washing; stuccoing; caulking; and canvas awning and ornamental iron installation." (F.S. 489.117 (2026) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.117)
  - What this means in practice: a driveway-only scope cannot be made to require a state or local license. A permit may still be required. The statute bars requiring a *license* to get the permit; it does not abolish the permit. Pavers and patios are not named. Slabs, footers, curbs and walls fall inside the 61G4-15.100(g) structural masonry scope. Whether a patio or paver job "substantially corresponds" to that scope is a judgment call the statute does not settle. (F.S. 489.117 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.117); (61G4-15.100 (LII) — https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61G4-15-100)
- Local licenses that survive under 489.117(4)(a)3. If a local government had the license before January 1, 2021, it may keep offering a license for "veneer, including aluminum or vinyl gutters, siding, soffit, or fascia; rooftop painting, coating, and cleaning above three stories in height; or fence installation and erection." Under (4)(a)2, counties that contain an Area of Critical State Concern may keep pre-2021 licenses. (F.S. 489.117 (2026) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.117)
- 489.117(2)(b) is a registration path for transitional local licensees. The board "shall issue a registration" with no exam to an applicant who held a state registration or a local license in a 489.105(3)(a)-(o) category "during 2021, 2022, or 2023." The applicant must also show that the local jurisdiction no longer offers that license type, and must meet the 489.115(5) insurance requirement. (F.S. 489.117 (2026) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.117)
- **HB 735 (2021) became ch. 2021-214, not ch. 2021-124**, effective July 1, 2021. It created F.S. 163.211: "The licensing of occupations is expressly preempted to the state." An exception kept local governments that licensed occupations before January 1, 2021, but "any such local government licensing of occupations expires on July 1, 2023." It also amended 489.117(4) and added the driveway and job-scope list. (Enrolled HB 735 (2021) — https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2021/735/BillText/er/PDF); (HB 735 bill page — https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2021/735)
- **The sunset date moved twice.** The current text of F.S. 163.211(2)(a) says pre-2021 local occupational licensing "expires on July 1, **2025**." The history notes cite ch. 2021-214, ch. 2023-271 and ch. 2024-212. As of 2026-10-01 that grandfather has expired. Section (2)(b) keeps "Any local government licensing of occupations authorized by general law." (F.S. 163.211 (2026) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/163.211)
- DBPR/CILB's own statement: "SB 1142 (2024) extended all local specialty contractor licensing until June 30, 2025. However, effective July 1, 2025, local governments may only license specialty contractor license types that substantially correspond to the specialty contractor license types offered by" the CILB, or that 489.117 expressly allows. It adds that local governments "may not require either a local or state license to obtain a permit for work if the work does not substantially correspond" to a CILB license type, and that DBPR will investigate building officials who do not comply. (DBPR CILB Hot Topics — https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/construction-industry/hot-topics/)
- Example of what was preempted, using Miami-Dade, which is outside our counties: Miami-Dade lists "Concrete Finishing," "Concrete Forming and Placing," "Concrete Slab Sawing and Core Drilling" and "Masonry Floor" among the local categories preempted after July 1, 2025. It cites HB 735 (2021), HB 1383 (2023) and SB 1142. (Miami-Dade Contractor License Category Preemption — https://www.miamidade.gov/global/license.page?Mduid_license=lic1648488034544767)
- **Manatee County** says on its current pages that it "issues and reciprocates Local Licenses" for "Mason, Masonry and Concrete, Garage Door Specialty Contractor, Concrete, Journeyman Electric, Journeyman Plumbing." State-certified contractors "have the option of registering." Neither page mentions HB 735. Manatee's concrete and masonry licenses presumably rest on correspondence to the state structural masonry category, but that is my inference and no page says so. (Manatee Contractor Licensing Section — https://www.mymanatee.org/departments/development-services-department/building-division/contractor-licensing-division); (Manatee Register Your Contractor License — https://www.mymanatee.org/services-and-amenities/service-listing/service-details/contractor-licensing)
- **Orange County** says building permits "may be obtained by a Florida licensed Certified or Registered General Contractor, Building Contractor or Residential Contractor," or by an owner-builder. The page lists no concrete license category. (Orange County Division of Building Safety — https://www.orangecountyfl.net/PermitsLicenses/DivisionOfBuildingSafety.aspx)
- Unlicensed contracts (F.S. 489.128) cannot be enforced *by the unlicensed contractor*. However, "if a state license is not required for the scope of work … the individual performing that work is not considered unlicensed," and a missing business tax receipt (ch. 205) does not make someone unlicensed. Under F.S. 713.02(7), unlicensed contractors get no lien rights. (F.S. 489.128 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.128); (F.S. 713.02 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/713.02)
- Exemptions in F.S. 489.103:
  - (9) Minor work exemption: work "less than $2,500," but not if the job is part of a larger operation or split up to evade the law, and never for a person who "advertises that he or she is a contractor."
  - (7) Owner-builder exemption.
  - (6) Finished products that do not become a permanent fixed part of the structure.
  - None of these exemptions names concrete, pavers or driveways.
  (F.S. 489.103 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.103)
- Pool contractor scope (489.105(3)(j)-(k)) covers "the construction, repair, and servicing of any swimming pool" and its equipment and interior finishes. The statute text does not mention pool *decks*. (F.S. 489.105 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.105)
- Building contractor scope (489.105(3)(b)) includes "remodeling, repair, or improvement of any size building if the services do not affect the structural members of the building." (F.S. 489.105 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.105)

## 2. How a homeowner can verify a contractor

- License lookup: DBPR's license search is at myfloridalicense.com/wl11.asp (I fetched it and it returned 200). Locally registered contractors' licensure and discipline information is also kept "available through the automated information system" under 489.117(4)(b). (DBPR License Search — https://www.myfloridalicense.com/wl11.asp); (F.S. 489.117 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.117)
- Insurance and financial responsibility (F.S. 489.115(5)(a)): before a certificate or registration is issued or renewed, the applicant files an affidavit that it "has obtained workers' compensation insurance as required by chapter 440, public liability insurance, and property damage insurance … in amounts determined by rule of the board." Initial certification also requires a credit report and proof of financial responsibility (5)(b). An applicant claiming a workers' comp exemption swears it will obtain the exemption within 30 days (5)(d). (F.S. 489.115 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.115)
- Rule 61G4-15.003 says that failing to "continually maintain liability and property damage insurance" is a violation. Proof of coverage is checked through random audits; the board asks for a Certificate of Insurance only when it requests one. (Fla. Admin. Code R. 61G4-15.003 (LII) — https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61G4-15-003)
- Workers' comp (F.S. 440.02):
  - In the construction industry, "employment" covers any private employment "in which one or more employees are employed." Outside construction the threshold is four or more employees.
  - At most "three officers of a corporation" in construction may elect an exemption. Each must own at least 10% of the stock and be listed as an officer with the Division of Corporations.
  - A construction "independent contractor" counts as an employee.
  (F.S. 440.02 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/440.02)
- Recovery fund notice (F.S. 489.1425): every residential repair or construction contract over $2,500 must include the "FLORIDA HOMEOWNERS' CONSTRUCTION RECOVERY FUND" statement. The fund pays "if you lose money … from specified violations of Florida law by a **licensed** contractor." The fine is up to $500 for a first violation and $1,000 for later ones. (F.S. 489.1425 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.1425)
- New in 2026: SB 290 was signed March 23, 2026 and took effect July 1, 2026. Licensed contractors must pay subcontractors and suppliers within 45 days after receiving payment, or as the contract says, unless there is a bona fide dispute. (DBPR CILB Hot Topics — https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/construction-industry/hot-topics/)

## 3. License number in advertising (F.S. 489.119(5))

- 489.119(5)(b): "The registration or certification number of each contractor shall appear in each offer of services, business proposal, bid, contract, or advertisement, regardless of medium, as defined by board rule, used by that contractor or business organization in the practice of contracting." (F.S. 489.119 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.119)
- 489.119(5)(c): if a vehicle carries the contractor's name, or anything suggesting it is used for contracting, the license number must be "conspicuously and legibly displayed." This does not create a mandatory vehicle-signage rule. (F.S. 489.119 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.119)
- 489.119(5)(d): stationery and promotional novelties are not "advertisement." (5)(e): the first offense gets a notice of noncompliance; a fine or citation follows if it is not fixed within 30 days. (F.S. 489.119 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.119)
- 489.119(5)(f): a local government may fine a person who is not certified or registered and who "claims to be licensed" in an ad or bid without holding a valid competency-based license, or who claims to be insured without being insured. (F.S. 489.119 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.119)

## 4. Lien law and homeowner protections

- **Threshold correction: F.S. 713.02(5) is $2,500, not $5,000.** "Any improvement for which the direct contract price is $2,500 or less shall be exempt from all other provisions of this part except the provisions of s. 713.05." (F.S. 713.02 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/713.02)
- Notice of Commencement (F.S. 713.13(1)(a)): "Except for an improvement that is exempt under s. 713.02(5), an owner … before actually commencing to improve any real property … shall record a notice of commencement in the clerk's office and post" a certified copy or a notarized statement. The NOC is void if work does not start within 90 days (713.13(2)). It expires 1 year after recording unless the notice states otherwise. The owner must sign it personally (713.13(1)(g)). (F.S. 713.13 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/713.13)
- **The $5,000 and $15,000 figures are in F.S. 713.135, not 713.02(5).**
  - Under 713.135(1)(e), the permitting authority requires a copy of the NOC to be filed "before the first inspection … if the direct contract is greater than $5,000."
  - The subsection "does not apply to a direct contract to repair or replace an existing heating or air-conditioning system in an amount less than $15,000."
  - Permit cards must carry the warning "YOUR FAILURE TO RECORD A NOTICE OF COMMENCEMENT MAY RESULT IN YOUR PAYING TWICE."
  (F.S. 713.135 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/713.135)
- Mandatory lien-law disclosure (F.S. 713.015): "Any direct contract greater than $2,500 between an owner and a contractor, related to improvements to real property consisting of single or multiple family dwellings up to and including four units" must include the construction-lien notice. It must be in at least 12-point, capitalized, boldface type, on the front page or a separate page, signed and dated by the owner. Leaving it out does not bar a lien against someone who was not adversely affected. (F.S. 713.015 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/713.015)
- Notice to Owner (F.S. 713.06(2)(a)): lienors not in privity with the owner, other than laborers, must serve a notice to owner "before commencing, or not later than 45 days after commencing, to furnish" labor or materials, and before final payment. Failing to serve it timely "is a complete defense to enforcement of a lien." (F.S. 713.06 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/713.06)
- Deposits over 10% (F.S. 489.126(2)): this applies to "without regard to the licensure of the person." A contractor who receives more than 10% of the residential contract price as an initial payment must apply for permits within 30 days. It must then start work within 90 days after permits issue, unless it has just cause or the owner agreed in writing to a longer period. Under (3), stopping work for 90 days after receiving more than the value of work done is prima facie evidence of a violation. (F.S. 489.126 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/489.126)
- 3-day cancellation (F.S. 501.021/501.025): this applies only to a "home solicitation sale" over $25, meaning the seller personally solicits away from its fixed business location. It **excludes** a sale "that results from a request for specific goods or services by the purchaser." In that case the buyer may cancel "until midnight of the third business day after" signing, by written notice. Business days exclude Sundays and federal holidays. So a homeowner who calls in or fills out a web form generally falls under the exclusion. (F.S. 501.021 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/501.021); (F.S. 501.025 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/501.025)
- Construction contracts have no general statutory cooling-off right beyond 501.025. DBPR notes that HB 715 (2025) added cancellation rights for *residential roof* contracts. That bill does not cover concrete or turf. (DBPR CILB Hot Topics — https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/construction-industry/hot-topics/)

## 5. HOAs, artificial turf and the DEP rule

- Architectural control (F.S. 720.3035):
  - An HOA's power to approve the "location, size, type, or appearance" of improvements exists "only to the extent that the authority is specifically stated or reasonably inferred" in the declaration or authorized published guidelines, and must be applied "reasonably and equitably."
  - If the documents list material or design options, the HOA may not restrict an owner's choice among them (2).
  - A denial must be in writing and must cite the specific rule and the nonconforming part (4)(a).
  - New in the 2026 statutes, ch. 2026-164: an HOA "may not require a building permit to be issued by a governmental authority to a parcel owner as a prerequisite for review" (1)(c).
  - The statute does not mention pavers or driveways.
  (F.S. 720.3035 (2026) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/720.3035)
- Items not visible from the street (F.S. 720.3045): regardless of covenants, and "unless prohibited by general law or local ordinance," an association "may not restrict parcel owners or their tenants from installing, displaying, or storing any items on a parcel which are not visible from the parcel's frontage or an adjacent parcel, an adjacent common area, or a community golf course, including, but not limited to, **artificial turf**." The protection covers only turf that is not visible. Front-yard turf visible from the frontage is not protected by this section. History: ch. 2023-64 and ch. 2024-221. (F.S. 720.3045 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/720.3045)
- **HB 683 (2025), ch. 2025-140, effective July 1, 2025**, created F.S. 125.572:
  - DEP must adopt minimum standards for synthetic turf "on single-family residential properties 1 acre or less in size."
  - Once the rules are adopted, a **local government** may not prohibit compliant turf or regulate turf "inconsistent with" DEP standards.
  - Ch. 2026-164 added (3)(b): the preemption "does not apply to … a community development district to enforce deed restrictions."
  - The statute regulates local governments only and says nothing about HOAs.
  (F.S. 125.572 (2026) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/125.572); (F.S. 125.572 (2025) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/125.572)
- The HB 683 final bill analysis (6/24/2025) says that before the bill, "Current law prohibits homeowners' associations from restricting … synthetic turf that is not visible from the parcel's frontage," and there was "no law restricting local governments from regulating synthetic turf." The bill was approved June 13, 2025. The same bill also bars building departments from requiring copies of contracts to issue permits. (HB 683 Final Bill Analysis — https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2025/683/Analyses/h0683z2.IPA.PDF)
- **DEP Rule 62-308.100 "Synthetic Turf": effective 5/19/2026 (CONFIRMED).** It was proposed 1/20/2026 and changed 3/17/2026. Authority: 125.572 FS. (FLRules 62-308.100 — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/ruleNo.asp?id=62-308.100)
  - Scope: "single-family residential properties of 1 acre or less." Local governments "may not regulate synthetic turf in a manner inconsistent with these minimum standards." The rule "does not establish nor require any new department-issued permit." It does not modify property rights, easements or rights of way. (Rule 62-308.100 text (.doc) — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)
  - Materials: turf, backing and infill "must not contain heavy metals or intentionally added per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances" and must be landfill-disposable. **Infill** may be only "clean silica sand, rock, shell, or other natural material," or coated silica sand with a non-toxic coating. Rubber or synthetic infill is allowed "only within the footprint of playground equipment." Installation must prevent infill from washing off the property. (Rule 62-308.100 text — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)
  - **Base**: "Subgrade shall be composed of natural materials, such as crushed rock, or crushed concrete … Subgrade materials shall be washed prior to installation to prevent fines from binding." (Rule 62-308.100(2)(d) — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)
  - Color: "Green synthetic turf shall be allowed." (Rule 62-308.100(3) — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)
  - **Permeability**: turf "must be permeable and affixed to permeable backing with a pervious subgrade." A local government "may establish a quantifiable standard of a maximum of 10 inches per hour for all layers." The site must be graded for positive drainage, and the soil must not be compacted enough to hurt percolation. (Rule 62-308.100(4) — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)
  - Stormwater: turf must not increase runoff volume or rates to neighbors. It may not alter a permitted stormwater system, and may not go "within a swale, ditch, stormwater pond, or a stormwater pond's littoral zone." (Rule 62-308.100(5) — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)
  - **Irrigation**: "In-ground irrigation systems cannot be used to irrigate synthetic turf areas." A local government may require existing heads to be removed and the pipe capped. (Rule 62-308.100(6) — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)
  - **Water setback**: local buffers may not be stricter than those for natural turf. Where no buffer exists, turf must be "no closer than 10 feet from a natural or man-made waterbody," measured from the ordinary or mean high water line. The exception is where a physical barrier such as a seawall or bulkhead exists. (Rule 62-308.100(7)(b) — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)
  - **Tree drip lines**: turf "shall not be installed inside tree drip lines, whether on the property or adjacent properties." The exceptions are trees that are noxious weeds under ch. 581, and cases where a "certified arborist … certifies that installation within that drip line would not be harmful." (Rule 62-308.100(8) — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)
  - Other requirements: install per the manufacturer's specifications; anchor all edges and seams against wind and flooding; keep septic tank access for pump-out; install landward of any dune system and never in place of dune vegetation. (Rule 62-308.100(9) — https://www.flrules.org/gateway/readFile.asp?sid=0&tid=30839130&type=1&file=62-308.100.doc)

# Part 4: Soil, climate, hazards, concrete/paver specs, water rules (researched 2026-10-01)

Only facts read in the cited source are recorded. "UNVERIFIED / NOT FOUND" marks anything I could not confirm from a primary source.

## 1. Dominant soils (USDA NRCS Official Series Descriptions)

### Orlando metro
- **Smyrna**: Sandy, siliceous, hyperthermic Aeric Alaquods. Poorly to very poorly drained. Permeability is rapid in the A, E and C horizons and moderate to moderately rapid in the Bh (spodic) horizon. Water table is less than 18 in. deep for 1–4 months in most years and 12–40 in. deep for more than 6 months. Texture is sand or fine sand, with some loamy sand in the Bh horizon. (NRCS OSD Smyrna — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/S/SMYRNA.html)
- **Myakka**: Sandy, siliceous, hyperthermic Aeric Alaquods. Very poorly or poorly drained, with "slow internal drainage and slow to ponded runoff". Water table is less than 18 in. deep for 1–4 months in most years and drops below 40 in. in dry periods. Sandy throughout. The OSD gives its extent as about 1,400,072 acres. (NRCS OSD Myakka — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/M/MYAKKA.html)
- **Candler**: Hyperthermic, uncoated Lamellic Quartzipsamments. Excessively drained, with very rapid to rapid permeability. Water table is deeper than 80 in. Sand and fine sand, with loamy sand lamellae below about 67 in. (NRCS OSD Candler — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/C/CANDLER.html)
- **Tavares**: Hyperthermic, uncoated Typic Quartzipsamments. Moderately well drained, with rapid to very rapid permeability. Seasonal high water table is 42–72 in. deep for more than 6–10 months in most years. Sand and fine sand, with about 5% silt plus clay. (NRCS OSD Tavares — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/T/TAVARES.html)
- **Astatula**: Hyperthermic, uncoated Typic Quartzipsamments. Excessively drained, with very rapid permeability. Saturation is more than 60 in. below the surface. Sand or fine sand with less than 5% silt plus clay. (NRCS OSD Astatula — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/A/ASTATULA.html)
- **Pomona**: Sandy, siliceous, hyperthermic Ultic Alaquods. Very poorly to poorly drained. Seasonal high water table is 0–12 in. from the surface for about 3 months in most years. Sand at the top, changing to sandy clay loam or sandy clay in the argillic horizon. (NRCS OSD Pomona — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/P/POMONA.html)
- **Basinger**: Siliceous, hyperthermic Spodic Psammaquents. Very poorly drained. Seasonal high water table is 0–18 in. Fine sand. Where undisturbed it is naturally ponded 6–9 months a year on low flats and drainageways. (NRCS OSD Basinger — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/B/BASINGER.html)

### Sarasota / Manatee
- **EauGallie**: Sandy, siliceous, hyperthermic Alfic Alaquods. Very poorly or poorly drained. Water table is within 6–18 in. for 1–4 months and within 40 in. for more than 6 months. Sand at the top, with a slowly permeable sandy loam to sandy clay loam Bt horizon at depth. (NRCS OSD EauGallie — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/E/EAUGALLIE.html)
- **Immokalee**: Sandy, siliceous, hyperthermic Arenic Alaquods. Poorly drained. Water table is 6–18 in. deep for 1–4 months and 18–42 in. deep for 2–10 months. Fine sand. (NRCS OSD Immokalee — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/I/IMMOKALEE.html)
- **Pomello**: Sandy, siliceous, hyperthermic Oxyaquic Alorthods. Somewhat poorly to moderately well drained, with moderately rapid permeability. Water table is 18–48 in. deep. Fine sand with 0–10% clay. (NRCS OSD Pomello — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/P/POMELLO.html)
- **Felda**: Loamy, siliceous, superactive, hyperthermic Arenic Endoaqualfs. Very poorly and poorly drained. Seasonal high water table is 0–12 in. for 2–6 months a year. Fine sand over a very slowly or slowly permeable sandy loam to sandy clay loam Btg horizon, with shelly fine sand below. (NRCS OSD Felda — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/F/FELDA.html)
- Myakka, described above, is also a dominant flatwoods soil in Sarasota and Manatee counties. (NRCS OSD Myakka — https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/M/MYAKKA.html)

### State soil and Spodosols
- **Myakka fine sand is VERIFIED as Florida's state soil.** "In 1989 the legislature designated Myakka fine sand as the official state soil." It is described as unique to Florida and found on more than 1.5 million acres of flatwoods, "the single most extensive soil in the state". (FL Dept. of State, State Soil — https://dos.fl.gov/florida-facts/florida-state-symbols/state-soil/)
- Spodosols cover about 8.4 million acres and are the most extensive soil order in Florida. Most Florida Spodosols are Aquods, "poorly drained Spodosols with a water table at or near the surface for much of the year". (UF/IFAS EDIS SL441 Agricultural Soils of Florida — https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/SS655)

### What this means for subgrade compaction
- ICPI says compaction of the soil subgrade "should be at least 98% of standard Proctor density as specified in ASTM D698". It warns that "this compaction standard may not be achievable in extremely saturated or very fine soils" and that stabilizing the subgrade may then be needed. (ICPI Tech Spec 2, 2020 — https://www.orco.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/ICPI-TechSpec2-ORCO.pdf)
- ICPI says soil "should be moist, without visible signs of water" when compacted, and that perforated drainage pipe (typically 4 in.) can be used in wet subgrades. (ICPI Tech Spec 2, 2020 — https://www.orco.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/ICPI-TechSpec2-ORCO.pdf)
- ICPI says "continually wet or weak soils will require that bases be at least 2 to 4 in. (50 to 100 mm) thicker." This applies directly to flatwoods soils with a water table within 18 in. (Myakka, Smyrna, Immokalee, EauGallie, Pomona, Felda, Basinger). (ICPI Tech Spec 2, 2020 — https://www.orco.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/ICPI-TechSpec2-ORCO.pdf)
- FBC Residential R506.2 says fill under slabs must be compacted for uniform support. Unless approved otherwise, fill may be no deeper than 24 in. for clean sand or gravel and 8 in. for earth. A 4 in. base of clean graded sand, gravel or crushed stone is required, with limited exceptions for well-drained soils. (2020 FBC Residential Ch. 5 via UpCodes — https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-residential-code-2020/chapter/5/floors)

## 4. Sinkholes and karst
- Florida Geological Survey: "Slightly acidic groundwater slowly dissolves cavities and caves in the limestone". **Cover-collapse** sinkholes form suddenly; **cover-subsidence** sinkholes form gradually. (FDEP/FGS Sinkhole FAQ — https://floridadep.gov/fgs/sinkholes/content/sinkhole-faq)
- FGS: "Since the entire state is underlain by carbonate rocks, sinkholes could theoretically form anywhere." Incidents are reported to the Subsidence Incident Reports database, and the FGS helpline is 850-245-2118. (FDEP/FGS Sinkhole FAQ — https://floridadep.gov/fgs/sinkholes/content/sinkhole-faq)
- Florida Senate Interim Report 2011-104 (Dec 2010), citing the Office of Insurance Regulation (OIR):
  - **Hernando, Pasco and Hillsborough** are the "so-called 'sinkhole alley' counties".
  - From 2006–2009, more than 66% of sinkhole claims were in those three counties.
  - More than 88% of claims were in eleven counties: Hernando, Pasco, Hillsborough, Pinellas, Marion, Polk, **Orange**, Alachua, Citrus, Miami-Dade and Broward.

  (FL Senate Interim Report 2011-104 — https://www.flsenate.gov/UserContent/Session/2011/Publications/InterimReports/pdf/2011-104bi.pdf)

Counties with a similar setting include **Orange**, Marion, Citrus and others, plus parts of **Lake**, **Seminole**, Sumter, Volusia and more. (FL Senate Interim Report 2011-104 — https://www.flsenate.gov/UserContent/Session/2011/Publications/InterimReports/pdf/2011-104bi.pdf)

## 5. Hot-weather concreting and curing
- ACI 305R defines hot weather as any combination of high ambient temperature, high concrete temperature, low relative humidity, high wind speed and solar radiation that impairs concrete quality. (NRMCA CIP 12 Hot Weather Concreting — https://www.nrmca.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/12pr.pdf)
- NRMCA CIP 12 hot-weather practice:
  - Avoid the hottest part of the day.
  - Do not sprinkle water on slabs to make finishing easier.
  - Avoid adding water to concrete more than 1½ hours old.
  - Dampen the subgrade without leaving ponded water.
  - Start curing once finishing is done and continue **at least 3 days**. White-pigmented curing compound reflects heat.
  - Chilled water can lower concrete temperature by up to 10°F; ice by up to 20°F.

  (NRMCA CIP 12 — https://www.nrmca.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/12pr.pdf)
- **ACI 301-20 and ACI 305.1-14 limit concrete temperature to 95°F (35°C) at the time of discharge.** Higher temperatures need documented field experience or testing. (ACI FAQ, Maximum temperature limits for hot-weather concreting — https://www.concrete.org/frequentlyaskedquestions.aspx?faqid=15)
- **Evaporation threshold of 0.2 lb/ft²/h (1 kg/m²/h)**: specifications require preventive steps when evaporation exceeds this rate. (ACI Concrete International, Snell & Radnaabazar, Mar 2007, "Estimating Evaporation Rates to Prevent Plastic Shrinkage Cracking" — https://www.concrete.org/publications/internationalconcreteabstractsportal/m/details/id/20064)
- NRMCA CIP 5 on plastic shrinkage: wind above 5 mph, low humidity and high temperatures raise evaporation. ACI 305R has a chart to estimate evaporation. Mitigations:
  - windbreaks and sunshades
  - fog sprays
  - dampened subgrade
  - evaporation retarder
  - synthetic fibers (ASTM C1116)
  - curing started early and kept moist for at least 3 days

  CIP 5 notes these cracks are usually parallel and 1–3 ft apart. (NRMCA CIP 5 — https://www.nrmca.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/05pr.pdf)
- ACI 308 curing duration: at daily mean temperatures above 40°F, the minimum curing period is **7 days or until 70% of specified strength, whichever is less**. This was confirmed only from secondary or reproduced copies, not from concrete.org itself, so treat it as UNVERIFIED against the current ACI 308R. (ACI 308R-01 copy — https://civillabs.kashanu.ac.ir/file/download/page/1621070592-308r-01.pdf-curing.pdf)
- **Unlike ACI 308, CIP 5 and CIP 12 say "at least 3 days"** of curing, not 7.

## 6. Residential flatwork norms
- **FBC Residential R506.1**: slab-on-ground floors follow ACI 332 and must be at least **3½ in. (89 mm)** thick. Compressive strength comes from R402.2. (2020 FBC Residential Ch. 5 — https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-residential-code-2020/chapter/5/floors)
- **FBC Residential Table R402.2**: interior slabs need **2,500 psi** in every weathering class. Porches, carports and garage floors need 2,500 psi (negligible weathering), 3,000 psi (moderate) or 3,500 psi (severe). Whether Florida counts as "negligible" weathering under Figure R301.2(1) is UNVERIFIED. (2020 FBC Residential Ch. 4 — https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-residential-code-2020/chapter/4/foundations)
- **FBC R506.2.3**: a 6-mil polyethylene vapor retarder with laps of at least 6 in. goes under slabs, with exceptions such as garages and unheated accessory structures. (2020 FBC Residential Ch. 5 — https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-residential-code-2020/chapter/5/floors)
- **NRMCA CIP 6 joints**:
  - Maximum joint spacing is **24 to 36 times slab thickness**. A 4 in. slab needs joints about 10 ft apart, and spacing should not exceed **15 ft**.
  - Panels should be nearly square, with length no more than 1.5× width. Avoid L-shaped panels.
  - Groove depth is at least ¼ of slab thickness and not less than 1 in.
  - Early-entry saw cuts are made 1–4 h after finishing; conventional saw cuts 4–12 h after.
  - Wire mesh "will not prevent cracking"; it keeps cracks from opening up.

  (NRMCA CIP 6 — https://www.nrmca.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/06pr.pdf)

## 7. Paver base specs (ICPI/CMHA)
- **Paver thickness**: "Pedestrian areas, driveways, and areas subject to limited vehicular use are paved with units 2 3/8 in. (60 mm) thick. Streets and industrial pavements should be paved with units at least 3 1/8 in. (80 mm) thick." (ICPI Tech Spec 2, 2020 — https://www.orco.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/ICPI-TechSpec2-ORCO.pdf)
- **Base thickness after compaction**:
  - Sidewalks and patios: at least **4 in. (100 mm)** over well-drained soils.
  - Residential driveways on well-drained soils: at least **6 in. (150 mm)**.
  - Wet or weak soils: 2–4 in. thicker.
  - Residential streets: 8–10 in.

  (ICPI Tech Spec 2, 2020 — https://www.orco.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/ICPI-TechSpec2-ORCO.pdf)
- **The common "8–12 in. for driveways" figure is not what ICPI says.** Tech Spec 2 says 6 in. on well-drained soils, plus 2–4 in. on wet soils.
- **Base compaction**:
  - Lifts of 4–6 in. with a 7,000 lbf reversible plate compactor.
  - Pedestrian areas and driveways: at least 98% of standard Proctor.
  - Vehicular areas: at least 98% of modified Proctor.
  - Surface tolerance: ±3/8 in. over 10 ft.

  (ICPI Tech Spec 2, 2020 — https://www.orco.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/ICPI-TechSpec2-ORCO.pdf)
- **Bedding sand** is screeded to an uncompacted nominal **1 in. (25 mm)**. Joints between pavers are at most 1/8 in. Dry joint sand is swept in and compacted. Sealers or joint sand stabilizers are optional and refer to Tech Spec 5. (ICPI Tech Spec 2, 2020 — https://www.orco.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/ICPI-TechSpec2-ORCO.pdf)
- **Edge restraints (Tech Spec 3)**:
  - Required along the perimeter and wherever the pavement material changes.
  - Plastic or aluminum restraints work on flexible or rigid bases; steel only on rigid bases.
  - Spikes go into the aggregate base, not the soil.
  - "Edging for planting beds and flower gardens is not an acceptable restraint."

  (ICPI Tech Spec 3, 2020 — https://www.angeluspavingstones.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/AngelusCoverSheet-TechSpec3.pdf)
- **Efflorescence (Tech Spec 5)**:
  - It is a whitish calcium carbonate deposit and "does not affect the structural performance or durability of concrete pavers".
  - It may peak "in as short as 60 days after installation" and some wears away.
  - When installed in the dry season, it may not show until the next wet weather.

  (ICPI Tech Spec 5, 2020 — https://www.angeluspavingstones.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/AngelusCoverSheet-TechSpec5.pdf)
- **Sealing interval (Tech Spec 5)**: no fixed number of years is given. Sealers "require reapplication after a period of wear and weather", depending on use, climate and sealer quality. Acrylics "generally last for a few years ... before re-coating is required". (ICPI Tech Spec 5, 2020 — https://www.angeluspavingstones.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/AngelusCoverSheet-TechSpec5.pdf)
- Polymeric sand: Tech Spec 5 covers joint sand stabilizers generally. A brand-specific polymeric sand spec was not looked up: UNVERIFIED / NOT FOUND.
